## 1. LANDMARK TRIALS: 30-SECOND ROUNDING PITCHES
These bite-sized pitches are structured specifically for rapid verbal retrieval when put on the spot by an attending consultant.

### 🔬 FEAST Trial (2011)
*   **Full Citation**: Maitland K, Kiguli S, Opoka RO, et al. Mortality after fluid bolus in African children with severe infection. N Engl J Med. 2011;364(26):2483-2495.
*   **Core Finding**: Proved that rapid fluid boluses (20-40 mL/kg) significantly increase 48-hour mortality by 45% in sub-Saharan African children with severe infection and impaired perfusion, compared to conservative maintenance fluids without a bolus.
*   **30-Second Rounding Pitch**: *"When the consultant asks about resuscitation in febrile children, cite the FEAST trial. Note that rapid fluid boluses increased relative mortality by 45% because in the absence of mechanical ventilation, aggressive volume expansion overrides the cardiac reserve of a reductively adapted, hyperdynamic heart."*
*   **Direct Source URL**: [https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa1101549](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa1101549)

---
### 🔬 AMBITION-cm Trial (2022)
*   **Full Citation**: Jarvis JN, Lawrence DS, Meya DB, et al. Single-dose liposomal amphotericin B treatment for cryptococcal meningitis. N Engl J Med. 2022;386(12):1109–1120.
*   **Core Finding**: Demonstrated that a single high-dose of liposomal amphotericin B (10 mg/kg) on Day 1, combined with 14 days of oral flucytosine and fluconazole, is non-inferior to the older 7-day deoxycholate regimens, while significantly reducing drug-induced anemia, nephrotoxicity, and clinical monitoring costs.
*   **30-Second Rounding Pitch**: *"On rounds, cite the AMBITION-cm trial to justify our first-line cryptococcal meningitis induction. A single high-dose of liposomal amphotericin B (10 mg/kg) on Day 1 is safer, cheaper, and non-inferior to deoxycholate regimens, making it our gold standard."*
*   **Direct Source URL**: [https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2111904](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2111904)

---
### 🔬 GASTROSAM Trial (2021)
*   **Full Citation**: Maitland K, Gastroenteritis Rehydration Of children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (GASTROSAM): A Phase II Randomised Controlled trial. Wellcome Open Res. 2021;6:160.
*   **Core Finding**: Challenged the traditional conservative WHO guidelines that prohibit intravenous fluid rehydration in severely acutely malnourished (SAM) children. Showed that cautious intravenous rehydration (100 mL/kg of standard isotonic Ringer's Lactate over 3 to 8 hours depending on dehydration severity) is safe and significantly improves survival.
*   **30-Second Rounding Pitch**: *"When asked about managing dehydrated children with severe acute malnutrition, cite the GASTROSAM trial. It proved that cautious intravenous rehydration (100 mL/kg Ringer's Lactate) is clinically safe, does not trigger heart failure, and improves survival over standard oral ReSoMal."*
*   **Direct Source URL**: [https://wellcomeopenresearch.org/articles/6-160/v1](https://wellcomeopenresearch.org/articles/6-160/v1)

---
### 🔬 Magpie Trial (2002)
*   **Full Citation**: The Magpie Trial Collaboration Group. Do women with pre-eclampsia, and their babies, benefit from magnesium sulphate? Lancet. 2002;359(9321):1877–1888.
*   **Core Finding**: Established that magnesium sulphate (MgSO4) reduces the relative risk of eclampsia by 58% in women with severe pre-eclampsia, and lowers the risk of maternal death, without causing significant maternal or neonatal adverse effects.
*   **30-Second Rounding Pitch**: *"To justify our aggressive seizure prophylaxis in pre-eclampsia, cite the Magpie trial. It proved that magnesium sulphate cuts the risk of eclampsia by 58%, making it our primary first-line intervention in the obstetric emergency department."*
*   **Direct Source URL**: [https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(02)08778-0/fulltext](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(02)08778-0/fulltext)

---
### 🔬 CRASH-2 Trial (2010)
*   **Full Citation**: CRASH-2 collaborators. Effects of tranexamic acid on death, vascular occlusive events, and blood transfusion in trauma patients with significant haemorrhage: a randomised, placebo-controlled trial. Lancet. 2010;376(9734):23-32.
*   **Core Finding**: Proved that early administration of Tranexamic Acid (TXA) (1 g loading dose over 10 minutes, followed by 1 g infusion over 8 hours) significantly reduces all-cause mortality in bleeding trauma patients, with the greatest benefit achieved when given within 3 hours of injury.
*   **30-Second Rounding Pitch**: *"In major trauma resuscitations, cite the CRASH-2 trial. It established that administering 1 g of TXA within 3 hours of injury significantly reduces bleeding-related mortality, meaning we must prioritize prompt delivery directly in the resuscitation bay."*
*   **Direct Source URL**: [https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(10)60835-5/fulltext](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(10)60835-5/fulltext)

## 2. CORE ADAPTED PROTOCOLS (Consensus vs. African Reality)

### 📌 Sepsis & Fluid Resuscitation (SSC vs. ASAP Protocol)
*   **Standard consensus (SSC)**: Aggressive fluid bolus of $\ge 30\text{ mL/kg}$ within the first 3 hours, rapid serum lactate tracking, and immediate blood cultures [407].
*   **The African Reality**: Widespread lack of mechanical ventilation and intensive monitoring means aggressive boluses precipitate fatal respiratory failure. The landmark **FEAST trial** proved rapid fluid boluses increase relative 48-hour mortality by **45%** [154]. Only **1.2%** of facilities have the resources to implement the complete SSC guidelines [154].
*   **Local Adaptation**: Replace the 1-hour bundle with the **ASAP (Assess, Stabilise, Administer, Prioritise) Protocol** [24]. Fluid resuscitation is limited to small, targeted micro-boluses (**4 mL/kg or 250-500 mL**) [156]. Lactate is replaced by serial **Capillary Refill Time (CRT)** monitoring [156], and fluid responsiveness is tracked dynamically using **Passive Leg Raise (PLR)** maneuvers [156].
*   **Attending Pitch**: *"In our resource-limited setting, aggressive fluid boluses are contraindicated because we lack mechanical ventilation to manage fluid overload. Following the FEAST trial, we resuscitate with 4 mL/kg micro-boluses and monitor perfusion dynamically using capillary refill time and passive leg raise maneuvers."*
*   **Active Bibliography & URL**:
    *   *Maitland K, et al. FEAST Trial.* N Engl J Med. 2011;364(26):2483-2495. URL: [https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa1101549](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa1101549)
    *   *Omullo FP, et al. ASAP Sepsis Framework.* Preprints.org. 2026. URL: [https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.0435](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.0435)

### 📌 Selective Trauma Management (ACS-TQP vs. SNOM)
*   **Standard consensus (ACS-TQP)**: Mandatory surgical exploration of any neck trauma penetrating the platysma.
*   **The African Reality**: Universal surgical exploration overwhelmed theaters and resulted in high rates of negative, non-therapeutic laparotomies.
*   **Local Adaptation**: **Selective Non-Operative Management (SNOM)**. Immediate surgery is reserved strictly for hemodynamically unstable patients or those exhibiting clinical **'hard signs'** of vascular or aerodigestive damage (e.g., active bleeding, expanding hematoma, air bubbling) [126, 158]. Stable patients undergo high-resolution **CT Angiography (CTA)**, reducing negative exploration rates to a mere **4.1%** [126, 161].
*   **Attending Pitch**: *"We practice Selective Non-Operative Management (SNOM) for penetrating neck trauma. Stable patients without clinical 'hard signs' undergo CT angiography instead of immediate surgery, reducing non-therapeutic neck explorations to only 4.1%."*
*   **Active Bibliography & URL**:
    *   *Penetrating Neck Trauma Audit.* South African Journal of Surgery. 2020;57(4). URL: [https://journals.co.za/doi/abs/10.36303/SAJS.4020](https://journals.co.za/doi/abs/10.36303/SAJS.4020)

### 📌 Pediatric Severe Acute Malnutrition (WHO vs. GASTROSAM)
*   **Standard consensus (WHO standard)**: Strictly prohibits intravenous fluid resuscitation in malnourished children with severe dehydration unless they present in advanced hypovolemic shock, fearing congestive heart failure. Mandates low-sodium oral ReSoMal.
*   **The African Reality**: ReSoMal is frequently out of stock [226], and oral-only rehydration in severe gastroenteritis leads to high mortality.
*   **Local Adaptation**: Cautious intravenous rehydration using standard isotonic **Ringer's Lactate (100 mL/kg over 3 to 8 hours)** as validated by the **GASTROSAM trial** is safe, does not precipitate congestive cardiac failure, and improves survival [227].
*   **Attending Pitch**: *"While traditional WHO guidelines restrict IV fluids in SAM, the GASTROSAM trial proved that cautious intravenous rehydration using 100 mL/kg Ringer's Lactate is safe, does not trigger cardiac failure, and significantly improves survival."*
*   **Active Bibliography & URL**:
    *   *Maitland K, et al. GASTROSAM Phase II Trial.* Wellcome Open Res. 2021;6:160. URL: [https://wellcomeopenresearch.org/articles/6-160/v1/pdf?article_uuid=f430258f-2d4f-436d-8507-3f238e55e4b9](https://wellcomeopenresearch.org/articles/6-160/v1/pdf?article_uuid=f430258f-2d4f-436d-8507-3f238e55e4b9)

### 📌 Severe Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia Resuscitation (ACOG vs. ESMOE)
*   **Standard consensus (ACOG)**: Intensive intravenous vasodilator infusions (e.g., continuous labetalol) and detailed electronic fetal monitoring.
*   **The African Reality**: Heavy infusion equipment is unavailable in secondary departments, and aggressive fluid therapy triggers fatal pulmonary edema, which accounts for over **30%** of maternal eclampsia deaths in South Africa.
*   **Local Adaptation**: The **ESMOE Protocol** structures resuscitation systematically around the **'Big Five' maternal systems** to stabilize the mother *before* any fetal checks [159]. Hypertension is managed with oral **rapid-acting Nifedipine (10 mg tablet, swallowed whole)**, and total fluid intake is strictly restricted to **80 mL/hour** to prevent pulmonary edema [159].
*   **Attending Pitch**: *"To prevent eclampsia-associated maternal death, we prioritize the mother's cardiovascular stability using the ESMOE Big Five approach. We manage severe hypertension with oral nifedipine and restrict total fluid intake strictly to 80 mL/hour to prevent fatal pulmonary edema."*
*   **Active Bibliography & URL**:
    *   *Dr Mandondo. ESMOE Eclampsia Guidelines.* Knowledge Hub. 2024. URL: [https://knowledgehub.health.gov.za/system/files/2024-04/ECLAMPSIA%20%20Dr%20MANDONDO%2020%20MARCH%202024.pdf](https://knowledgehub.health.gov.za/system/files/2024-04/ECLAMOSIA%20%20Dr%20MANDONDO%2020%20MARCH%202024.pdf)
